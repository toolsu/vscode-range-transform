Run **Range & Transform: Insert Sequence from Range** and type a range:

```text
start:stop:step
```

- **`start`** is required and decides the _kind_ of sequence (see the table below).
- **`stop`** is optional. Leave it out and the sequence keeps counting: `1:` fills every
  cursor, `mon::2` gives every other weekday.
- **`step`** is optional and defaults to `1`. Its sign is corrected for you, so `10:1`
  counts down without needing `-1`.

With several cursors, one item goes to each cursor and the sequence stops there. With a
single cursor there is nowhere to spread it, so the whole sequence is inserted joined by
newlines: put one cursor down, type `1:10`, and get ten lines.

<Aside type="tip">
Want to try a range before installing? The [Sequence Generator](https://toolsu.com/convnum/tools/sequence-generator/)
runs the same convnum conversions in your browser.
</Aside>

## What `start` can be

Anything [convnum](https://toolsu.com/convnum/) recognises: 26 numeral systems plus dates, each counted and written
back in its own terms.

| Zero-padded decimal | `01:05` | `01` `02` `03` `04` `05` |

| English ordinal abbreviations | `1st:5th` | `1st` `2nd` `3rd` `4th` `5th` |

## Things worth knowing

- **The style of `start` is preserved.** `MON:FRI` stays upper case, `0XA:0XF` keeps its
  upper-case prefix, `01:10` keeps its leading zero, and `一:十` stays Simplified.
- **Cyclic kinds wrap around.** `fri::2` continues past Sunday into the next week, and
  `甲::3` wraps through the ten stems.
- **`step` can be written in any base notation.** `0x0A::0x10` steps by sixteen, the
  same as `0x0A::16`.
- **`start` and `stop` must be the same kind.** `Mon:5` produces nothing, and the input
  box tells you so before you press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Watch out for pairs that share a
  kind you did not have in mind: `1` and `a` are both hexadecimal digits, so `1:a` counts
  `1` `2` … `9` `a` rather than failing.
- **Roman numerals versus letters.** `I`, `V`, `X`, `C` and friends are also perfectly
  good Latin letters, and a single letter is read as a letter. Write at least one end
  with two or more characters (`II:X` or `I:XX`) to get Roman numerals.
- **Month and weekday names work in about forty languages.** `janvier:juin`,
  `Januar:Juni`, `enero:junio`, `январь:июнь`, `ocak:haziran`, `1月:6月` and
  `星期一:星期五` are each counted and written back in their own language, keeping the
  case and the long or short form you typed. A name that several languages share is
  settled by the other end of the range where possible: `mars:juin` is French, `mars:maj`
  is Swedish. With nothing to go on, the more widely used language wins.
- **Chinese numerals are read in their canonical form.** `一万` counts in ordinary
  numerals and `壹万` in the financial ones. A bare `万` is neither (no Chinese numeral
  is written that way), so it is not recognised, and nor is `一十` in place of `十`.

## Settings that affect ranges

`rangeTransform.defaultSequenceLength` (default `10`) is how many items a single cursor
gets when the range has no `stop`. `rangeTransform.rememberLastInput` pre-fills the
input box with the last range you used.
